# Dating, Commitment, and Mismatched Timelines

> SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE — written for CoupleCare AI development. Not authoritative guidance and not a substitute for professional support.

## Different speeds

Partners often move towards commitment at different speeds. One may be ready to talk about exclusivity, moving in, or marriage while the other is unsure. A difference in timing does not automatically mean a difference in feelings, but it does need an honest conversation.

## Asking directly

Uncertainty is easier to live with when it is spoken. "I'd like to understand where you see us going. I'm not asking for a decision tonight; I want to know how you're thinking about it." Asking for their perspective, rather than demanding a commitment, makes an honest answer more likely.

## Listening to the answer

People sometimes hear what they hope rather than what was said. If a partner repeatedly says they are not ready, or avoids the subject over a long time, it is reasonable to take that at face value and consider what you need. Waiting can be a valid choice; so can deciding the timelines are too different.

## Family and cultural expectations

In many families, decisions about commitment and marriage involve parents and relatives. This can bring support and also pressure. It helps when partners first agree between themselves about what they want, and then decide together how to communicate with their families.

## Red flags early on

Early warning signs worth noticing include pressure to commit very quickly, extreme jealousy presented as romance, isolating someone from friends, and disrespect for "no". These matter more than whether someone texts back quickly.
