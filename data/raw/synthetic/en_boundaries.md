# Boundaries and Expectations

> SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE — written for CoupleCare AI development. Not authoritative guidance and not a substitute for professional support.

## What a boundary is

A boundary describes what you will and will not do, or what you need in order to feel respected and safe. It is about your own actions, not about controlling another person. "I will leave the conversation if we start shouting" is a boundary. "You are not allowed to see your friends" is an attempt to control someone else.

## Unspoken expectations

Many conflicts come from expectations that were never said out loud: how often to call, how money is shared, how much time is spent with family, how household work is divided, how affection is shown. People often assume their own expectations are obvious and "normal". Making them explicit turns a vague resentment into something that can be negotiated.

## Stating a boundary

A clear boundary is specific, calm, and includes what you will do. "I'm happy to visit your parents twice a month. More than that leaves me exhausted, so I'll stay home on the other weekends." It does not need a long justification, and it does not require the other person to agree that it is reasonable.

## When boundaries are repeatedly ignored

If a partner repeatedly ignores a boundary, the pattern matters more than any single incident. It may be worth asking whether they understand it, whether they disagree with it, or whether they are dismissing it. Persistent disregard for boundaries — particularly around privacy, sex, money, or contact with others — can be part of a controlling dynamic.

## Consent

Sexual boundaries deserve particular clarity. Consent must be freely given and can be withdrawn at any time, including within marriage. Pressure, guilt, threats, or persistence until someone gives in are not consent.
