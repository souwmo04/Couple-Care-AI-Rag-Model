# When the Feelings Are One-Sided

> SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE — written for CoupleCare AI development. Not authoritative guidance and not a substitute for professional support.

## It really hurts

Having a crush on someone who doesn't like you back — unrequited or one-sided love — or who has not noticed you at all, can be painful, distracting and lonely. These feelings are common and they say more about your capacity to care than about any failing in you. It helps to treat yourself with the kindness you would offer a friend in the same position.

## Deciding whether to say something

Telling someone how you feel can bring relief and clarity, even if the answer is no. Before you do, ask yourself whether you can accept any answer, and whether the timing is fair to them — for example, not while they are in a relationship or in the middle of a difficult time.

## How to say it

Keep it honest, brief and free of pressure: "I've really enjoyed getting to know you, and I've started to have feelings for you. I wanted to be honest. There's no pressure at all — I'll be fine whatever you feel." This leaves them free to answer truthfully.

## Accepting the answer

If they do not feel the same, thank them for their honesty and give them — and yourself — some space. Do not keep asking, try to change their mind, test them with jealousy, or monitor their social media. Respecting a no protects both people and often preserves the friendship.

## Staying friends — or not

Some people can stay close friends after unreturned feelings; others need distance to heal. Both are reasonable. If seeing them, or their posts, keeps the pain sharp, muting or reducing contact for a while is self-care, not rudeness.

## Looking after yourself

Spend time with people who make you feel valued, return to interests that absorb you, and allow the feelings to fade at their own pace. If the sadness stays heavy for a long time, affects sleep or study, or turns into hopelessness, talk to someone you trust or a counsellor.
