# Feeling Unheard: Listening and Speaking in a Relationship

> SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE — written for CoupleCare AI development. Not authoritative guidance and not a substitute for professional support.

## Why feeling unheard hurts

When one partner feels that the other does not listen, the hurt is usually less about the topic and more about the sense of not mattering. A person may repeat themselves, raise their voice, or withdraw, and each of these can make the other partner more defensive. Neither partner is necessarily acting in bad faith; often both are trying to be understood at the same time and nobody is in the listening role.

## Possible reasons a partner seems not to listen

There are many possible explanations, and only the two people involved can find out which apply. A partner may be distracted by stress at work or by a phone. They may jump to problem-solving when the other person wanted empathy first. They may feel criticised and stop taking in information. Some people need time to process before they respond. In some relationships, not listening is part of a wider pattern of dismissiveness, and that deserves to be taken seriously too.

## Choosing a good moment

Important conversations go better when both people are reasonably calm, fed, and not rushed. Asking "Is now a good time to talk about something that matters to me?" gives the other person a chance to arrive. If the answer is no, agreeing on a specific later time keeps the conversation from being postponed indefinitely.

## Speaking about your experience

Describing your own experience is usually easier to hear than describing the other person's character. Compare "You never listen" with "When I was telling you about my day and you looked at your phone, I felt like what I said didn't matter to you." The second version names a specific moment, the feeling, and the need, without a verdict about who the partner is.

## Reflective listening

When it is your turn to listen, try summarising what you heard before responding: "So it sounds like you felt alone when I kept checking my phone. Did I get that right?" Reflecting does not mean agreeing. It shows that the message arrived, which often lowers the temperature of the conversation enough for problem-solving to begin.

## A short script to try

"I'd like us to talk about something that's been on my mind. I've been feeling unheard lately, especially in the evenings. I'm not trying to blame you — I want us to find a way to feel closer. Could you tell me how things have felt from your side?"
