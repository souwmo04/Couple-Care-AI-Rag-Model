# Recognising Abuse and Staying Safe

> SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE — written for CoupleCare AI development. Not authoritative guidance and not a substitute for professional or emergency support.

## Conflict versus abuse

Ordinary conflict involves two people who disagree but can both express themselves without fear. Abuse is a pattern in which one person uses fear, control, or harm to dominate the other. Abuse can be physical, emotional, sexual, financial, or digital, and it can happen in dating relationships and in marriages.

## Coercive control

Coercive control describes a pattern of behaviours that restrict a person's freedom: monitoring their phone or location, controlling money, isolating them from friends and family, constant criticism or humiliation, threats, and making them afraid of the partner's reactions. Each incident may look small; the pattern is what matters.

## If you are in immediate danger

If you are in immediate danger, contact your local emergency number or go to a safe place with other people. A chatbot cannot call for help or know where you are.

## Safety planning

Specialised domestic-violence services can help people plan for safety, whether they are staying, preparing to leave, or have left. A safety plan may include a safe person to contact, a code word, copies of important documents, and a place to go.

## Why couples counselling may not be appropriate

When one partner is abusive, joint sessions can put the other person at risk, because what they say may later be used against them. Specialised services usually recommend individual support first.

## Supporting someone else

If someone tells you they are being harmed, believing them, avoiding judgement, and respecting their decisions helps more than telling them what to do.
